## Section 3. Story

**Why this brand exists.** The after-meal paan ritual is 2,000 years old and the category traded it for a tobacco hit and a stain on the wall. The local paan-wala stopped making the eat-and-swallow version a long time ago. The Paan Legacy makes the version your grandmother would still recognise — hand-rolled, named ingredients, tobacco-free, supari-free, meant to be eaten, not spat.

**Who it's for.** Tier-1 and Tier-2 Indian families, 32–48, household income ₹15L+, who want the ritual back without the tobacco.

**The wedge.**
- Paan-and-mukhwas only — not a sweet shop that also sells paan.
- Named ingredients, roasted and rolled in-house — not bulk-sourced.
- Gift-ready packaging without being asked.

**Anti-positioning — what we will never do.**
- **Never compete on price.** Loose paan is ₹15. We are ₹179–299. We say the gap out loud and explain it; we never close it.
- **Never use the words *gourmet*, *artisanal*, *premium* or *authentic*.** The category cheapened them.
- **Never cut a corner on FSSAI.** Non-negotiable.

## Section 5. Customer

**Primary — the ritual restorer.** 35–50, metro household (Mumbai, Bengaluru, Delhi, Pune, Hyderabad). Had *stopped* buying paan because the local shop is tobacco-first. Buys to put the after-meal ritual back on the family table.

**The insight that should shape all copy: it is never eaten alone.** Nine reviews name another person in the house — husband, mother-in-law, mother, kids, partner. This is a product two generations eat at the same table. Write for the table, not the individual.

> *"This is the first paan my mother has eaten in fifteen years."* — R, Gurgaon
> *"Tobacco free is the reason my mother in law eats it with me."* — G, Delhi

**Secondary — the gifter.** In-laws, housewarmings, weddings, Diwali, Akshaya Tritiya. This is a **referral loop, not just a sale**: the recipient calls to ask the brand name, then buys. Gifting is the cheapest acquisition channel and it is happening unprompted.

**Emerging — the granola crossover.** Granola Goodness is a genuine front door. Documented path: enter on granola → end up on Meetha Paan.

**Discovery:** Zomato/Swiggy · receiving it as a gift · guests asking at events · the PDP claim itself (*"bought because the page said spit-free"*).

**Retention:** verifying the promise ("confirmed spit-free") · heritage recognition · the calendar — Sunday lunch, evening ritual. Repeat purchase rate **30%**, far above a category with almost no repeat at all.

**Customer's own words — use these, do not improve them:**
"eat and swallow" · "no tobacco no spit" · "grandmother's paan" · "my Sunday paan ritual is back" · "the packaging itself feels like a gift" · "hand roasted is not marketing copy here" · "the saffron isn't a gimmick" · "festival in a box"

**Known weaknesses — customers raise these before we do:**
- Summer melt on Almond Delight and Dry Fruit, Delhi/Hyderabad/Lucknow, May–August
- Mukhwas pack size, flagged twice
- East-zone delivery — 5 days to Kolkata
- **Subscribe-and-save demanded by 3x repeat buyers.** Demand exists before the feature. Interim: manual 6-week calendar reminder, no auto-payment.

### Voice rules

**The refrain — never paraphrase:** *"Eat, do not spit."* Paired with *"The leaf is leaf, not garnish."*

**ALWAYS**
- Short declaratives and fragments. *"Rose. Saffron. ₹299."* Almost no subordinate clauses.
- **Name the thing, don't adjective it.** *"Rose petals from the Pushkar belt."* *"Saffron, three threads per paan, the Kashmiri grade."* *"240gsm uncoated kraft."*
- **Negate, then assert.** *"The almond is not an almond essence; it is an almond."*
- **Say the price gap out loud, then downsell.** *"A loose paan from the street is ₹15. We are ten times that."* → *"If the price feels heavy, start with the Meetha Paan at ₹179."*
- **Family is the authority, never the brand.** *"My mother said 'this is not the paan I make' — and meant it as a compliment."*
- Instruct the customer. *"Take one out, eat it in one piece. Do not bite and put down."*
- Tell them to consume less. *"Two paans per person is the right number."*
- First person singular, signed. *"— Puneet." "I read it."*
- Admit weakness flat. *"A known weak spot, and we have been slow to solve it."*

**NEVER**
- *gourmet · artisanal · premium · authentic*
- *elevate · unlock · "in today's fast-paced world"* — AI tells
- *"premium"* as a standalone headline word
- Corporate hedging — *"committed to ongoing improvement"* is the named anti-pattern
- Upsell, cross-sell, "while you're here." One question, one answer, payment link or honest no.
- *natural / pure* — deprecated, tested 1.4x worse than "tobacco-free"

**Reading level:** Class 8. Short words, concrete nouns, no jargon.

**Sales policy (protect this — an AI will optimise it away):** We say no and lose the sale. Celiac kid → *"we would NOT recommend it"* → offered the cheaper mukhwas → got the sale and the trust. Honesty outranks conversion, always.

### FSSAI claim wall — hard limits

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Health-claim ceiling** | Nothing beyond *"traditionally taken after meals."* Hard stop. |
| **Gluten** | Granola Goodness contains oats and is **NOT certified gluten-free** — never describe it as gluten-free. Herbal Mukhwas **is** gluten-free. |
| **Allergens, declare per SKU** | Almond Delight: tree nuts (almond), cardamom, rose, fennel. Meetha Paan: cardamom, rose, fennel. |
| **Safe factual claims** | "Tobacco-free", "no supari", "no zarda", "vegetarian", "hand-rolled". Product facts, not health claims. |
| **Shelf life / storage** | Best within 14 days of dispatch. Refrigerate on receipt. |
| **License** | Number on box, manufacturing license on file, shared on request. |

> **🔴 Highest-risk trap.** Herbal Mukhwas is tagged `digestion` and is literally named *Herbal*. This is where a teammate will write *"aids digestion"* or *"good for gut health"* — both are over the wall. Same risk on Granola Goodness, which customers already feed their children. Flag any digestion, immunity, detox or gut-health phrasing before it ships.
