### One brief already existed, and it collides with mine on a single decision

`my-work/performance-marketer/briefs/2026-09-10-almond-delight-heritage-ritual.md` was already in the repo when this run started. It briefs the Almond Delight heritage ritual relight, which is exactly ad A4-1, the control in angle 4. It is a good brief and I have not touched it. Read it alongside mine, because on one point the two disagree and a designer handed both has been handed nothing.

**The disagreement:** that brief asks for **a second pair of hands, two people in frame, one offering and one receiving.** My angle 4 brief asks for **a second empty plate and no people at all.**

Both are reading the same Voice of Customer finding, Theme 3 at 8 of 27, and both are right that the second person is the point. The split is whether you cast the second person or imply them. Casting is a shoot with two models, a location and a half day. The empty plate is one afternoon with a camera and no people to book, and it cannot be miscast by age, which matters given the VoC report's warning that the 32 to 48 band is an ad-targeting assumption with no customer evidence behind it.

**My recommendation, given the account has been dark 22 days and the Diwali window opens on 5 October:** shoot the empty plate first because it can exist this week, and hold the two-hands version as the second creative once spend is flowing. That is a scheduling call, not a quality one. Pick one before briefing anybody.

## Top five reads

### 1. The strongest ad in the strongest angle

**`2026-09-10-angle-4-social-proof-the-table/meta.md`, ad A4-1.** Headline "Tobacco-free paan. Eat, do not spit."

Angle 4 is the strongest angle because it sits on the largest customer theme in the set, 8 of 27, and because its control ad is the only creative in this entire run with a measured performance result behind it rather than an inference. `CLAUDE.md` S7 records heritage ritual on Almond Delight as the best ROAS in the account, and it records the only A/B result the account has ever produced, "tobacco-free" beating "natural" and "pure" by 1.4x. A4-1 uses both. It matches the winners pattern on every axis, which makes it the control that the other nine ads are measured against.

The reasoning underneath: this brand does not currently need a clever new idea. It needs its best known creative switched back on. A4-1 is that, and it is written so the other four ads in the file each break it on exactly one axis, so whatever happens next is readable.

### 2. The brief ready to hand to a designer today

**`2026-09-10-angle-5-competitor-gap-corporate-diwali/creative-brief.md`.**

It asks for one photograph: five closed kraft boxes on a plain desk with a blank sleeve, flat daylight, no props. No shoot crew, no model, no food styling, no festival set build. The product it photographs already exists and the founder already ships it. The image prompt at the bottom is ready to paste into an image tool as it stands, and the negative prompt names 30 things that must not appear, most of which are exactly what every competitor in the window is currently putting in frame.

It carries one blocking item, in the pre-ship checklist, and it is a pricing decision rather than a creative one.

### 3. The ad most likely to move the metric the brand is weakest on

**A4-1 again, and the reason is worth reading, because it changes what "ship this week" means.**

`my-work/growth-analyst/2026-09-10-brief.md` is unambiguous about where the weakness is. The headline is that the Meta account has been dark for 22 days, 59 days before Diwali. The entire 90 day spend is ₹1,615.56, all of it in the single week ending 19 August, all of it on mukhwas prospecting. The alarm is Meta spend down effectively 100%. Its stated action is one line: **relight the heritage ritual creative on Almond Delight this week, before the 5 October Diwali window.**

So the weakest metric is not CAC and it is not LTV to CAC. Those are real problems, ₹475 against a ₹400 target and 2.5x against 2.9x, and the same brief dates both to 2026-05-12 and refuses to treat them as current. The live weakness is that **new customer acquisition is at zero**, and the repeat rate of 30% that the brand is proudest of only compounds on a pool that new customers refill.

That reframes this whole run. The job this week is not to launch the most interesting test in the file. It is to restore spend with the known-best creative, which is A4-1, and let exactly one probe run beside it. Everything else in these six files is October work.

The second consequence is a cost nobody has priced. 22 dark days means the relight re-enters Meta's learning phase, and the season this creative has to carry opens on 5 October, 25 days out. The learning has to happen before the season, not during it. That is the real bill for the silence, and it gets larger every day the answer to "was the stop deliberate" is unknown.

### 4. The angle that most exposes a competitor weakness

**Angle 5, and specifically ad A5-2, the employee gift.**

The Market Analyst report found competitors selling every occasion in the calendar while this brand sells one, and it found the corporate Diwali auction already crowded on 8 and 9 September. That looks like competitor strength. The weakness is underneath it.

Bombay Sweet Shop's Diwali collection, across every listing the analyst could see, is mithai, chocolate and dry fruit. **No paan.** They have to build a full seasonal creative set for every occasion they want to own. This brand's box, by the VoC report's count, already produces a phone call from the recipient four times in 27 messages with no creative behind it at all. One competitor buys the occasion. The other one gets phoned.

And the corporate filter the buyer actually applied, tobacco-free and vegetarian, is a filter a mithai and chocolate hamper cannot pass on the first term. That is a structural gap in a rival's product, not a claim about them, which is why no competitor is named anywhere in the copy.

## What I deliberately did not do

- **No Herbal Mukhwas creative, in either angle.** The Voice of Customer report is flat about it, "The fix is 300g or a lower price, not better copy", with 2 of 2 reviewers calling 200g small for ₹219. The Market Analyst report found rivals advertising 400g packs, a 5x100g combo, and an explicit "Mouth Fresheners Under ₹300" anchor in the same feed. The Content Lead gave it no calendar slot for the same reason and the Storefront Specialist and Marketplace Editor both stop at the same packaging decision. Writing ad copy against arithmetic is the one thing every teammate in this chain has independently refused to do. It is also the highest FSSAI risk SKU in the file, tagged `digestion` and named `Herbal`, against a rival advertising "Digestive Mouth Freshener" that this brand cannot match. **The uncomfortable part: the only rupees this account has spent in 90 days went to mukhwas prospecting.** The last ad budget went to the one product a better hook cannot rescue.
- **No summer shipping reassurance creative,** even though `CLAUDE.md` ranks it the number one creative gap by cost. The melt window is May to August and this is September, so it would be built seven months early. The VoC report also reframes it as possibly a transit-time problem wearing a heat problem's clothes, since both Hyderabad reviews are clean 5 stars and the only melt complaint is Delhi. The brief may change before the creative is needed. Build it in March.
- **No Masala Cranberry creative.** `CLAUDE.md` calls it last on every metric. The VoC report disagrees and names the real problem as a 1.9% conversion rate, which is a product page problem, not an ad problem. Paid traffic into a page that converts at 1.9% spends money to prove a point already proven.
- **No anti-positioning angle, so no price-gap ad.** "A loose paan from the street is ₹15. We are ten times that" is one of the strongest lines the brand owns and it is angle 3, not one of the two this run was sized for. It is also already scheduled organically on 19 September and 5 October. Re-run with `ANGLES=3` if the price objection needs paid air cover.
- **No child in any creative, and the strongest Theme 3 quote left unused because of it.** "The kids can even have a small bite" is the most persuasive sentence in the largest theme in the set. Almond Delight declares tree nuts. The Content Lead flagged the same tension on the organic Sunday post. It is the founder's call, not mine, and I did not make it quietly.
- **No video beyond one founder reel.** A4-3 is the only motion asset in the run and it is deliberately shootable on a phone in the kitchen, one continuous take, no cuts, no music. The team is small and hand-rolls product the morning of dispatch. There is no point handing a founder a video brief nobody can shoot.
- **No health claim, and nothing near one.** Nothing about digestion, gut health, immunity or detox appears in any of the six files except inside the flags telling the founder not to write it. The ceiling of "traditionally taken after meals" was available and is not used anywhere, because it does not fit a 30 character headline and no ad in this run needs it.
- **No competitor named and no comparative claim, in any of the 10 Meta ads or 6 Google sets.** Angle 5 exists entirely because of what competitors are doing, and none of it reached the copy. Every competitor price in the Market Analyst report is marked unverified, with three storefronts blocked by the egress proxy, so any comparison would have been unsourced. `CLAUDE.md` S6 separately forbids writing against Open Secret and TagZ, and the Market Analyst notes TagZ was acquired in a distress sale, so that comparison would be stale as well as banned. The competitive insight sits in the targeting and the timing. Never in the words.
- **No budget plan, no ROAS forecast, no CAC target per angle.** All three would have been invented. 62 clicks is not a sample.
