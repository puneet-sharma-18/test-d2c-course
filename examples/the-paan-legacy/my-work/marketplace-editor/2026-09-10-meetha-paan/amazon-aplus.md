## Module 1, image and body, 250 words

**Image:** one paan out of its wrapper on a plain white plate, shot close, leaf edges visible. Second frame, the open box of six.

**Headline:** The paan you finish, not the one you spit.

**Body**

Most paan sold in India is built around tobacco and supari. You chew it, you spit it, and you leave a stain on a wall. That version is cheap, it is everywhere, and it is not what we make.

This is the meetha paan the adults of the house used to eat after Sunday lunch. Rose. Gulkand. Fennel. Cardamom. A thread of coconut. Wrapped in a betel leaf that you swallow, because the leaf is leaf, not garnish.

No tobacco. No supari. No zarda. Vegetarian.

We roll it by hand in a small Mumbai kitchen on the morning it is dispatched. It is not made to stock. Six paan to a box, each one wrapped separately.

**How to eat it.** Take one out and eat it in one piece. Do not bite it and put it down. Do not spit. If it is your first time, eat half and drink water.

**On the price.** A loose paan from a street stall is around ₹15. This is ₹179 for six. We are not closing that gap and we are not going to pretend it is not there. What it buys is a named ingredient list, an FSSAI licensed kitchen, and something a child or a grandmother can eat at the same table.

Refrigerate on arrival. Best within 14 days of dispatch. Traditionally taken after meals.

## Module 3, brand story, 150 words

I sold my family's paan shop in Kanpur to start this.

The reason is simple. The shop had stopped making the version I grew up with. The category traded a two thousand year old after-meal habit for a tobacco hit and a stain on the wall, and nobody was making the paan my grandmother would still recognise.

So we make that one. Hand-rolled, named ingredients, tobacco-free, in a small kitchen in Mumbai. Six to a box, rolled the morning they go out.

Back to the specification. Rose, gulkand, fennel, cardamom, coconut, betel leaf. Vegetarian. Allergens are cardamom, rose and fennel. Refrigerate on arrival, best within 14 days of dispatch, FSSAI licence number printed on every box.

Puneet, The Paan Legacy

## SAFETY FLAGS

1. **`{almond-price}` in bullet 4 is a placeholder.** The figure is ₹299 in `CLAUDE.md`, but only fill it if the Amazon listing price will match the site price. Cross-listing a different price in a bullet is how listings get suppressed.
2. **Net weight is missing and Amazon requires it for a food listing.** Nothing on file states the gram weight of a box of six. This blocks the listing.
3. **FSSAI licence number is not in any file.** The listing says the number is on the box and does not state it. Amazon's food category will ask for it at upload. Real number or the listing does not go live.
4. **No Amazon restricted words used.** No "best", "guaranteed", "miracle", no medical or health claim. The only after-meal language is "traditionally taken after meals", verbatim from the claim wall.
5. **No named competitor.** The comparison column says "typical paan from a stall", a category, not a brand.
6. **The two thousand year claim** appears in the brand's own live PDP and ad copy and is carried through here. It is a cultural statement rather than a product claim, so it is low risk, but it is inherited, not sourced.
