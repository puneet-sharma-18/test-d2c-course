# Creative Brief, The Box That Gets A Phone Call
Date: 2026-09-10
Surface: Meta feed and Instagram feed, static. One asset doubles as the WhatsApp broadcast image and the corporate enquiry header.
Format: 1080x1080 and 1080x1350, JPG or PNG. One 1600x900 crop for the enquiry page header.

## The angle in one sentence

Photograph the box closed, because the box is the ad and it already works without one.

## Why now

Two independent findings point at the same hole.

**From the customer side.** Voice of Customer Theme 4, "the box recruits the next customer", is **6 of 27, and the loop is documented four separate times**: a gift is received, the recipient phones to ask the brand name, the recipient buys. One of those four became an 80 box corporate order. The report's line is that gifting is not a seasonal SKU line, it is the acquisition engine, and it is running unmanaged. Source: `my-work/voice-of-customer/2026-09-09-voc-report.md`.

**From the competitive side.** `my-work/market-analyst/2026-09-09-intel-report.md` found **13 of 25 ads in a corporate Diwali search live on 8 and 9 September, one targeting Bangalore companies by name**, and Bombay Sweet Shop running at least 13 creatives across occasions this brand does not build for. Its ship-in-30-days instruction was one line: the corporate gifting creative, WhatsApp-first, Bengaluru-led. It is the only gap where this brand has proven operational capability and no demand generation at all.

**And the clock.** Diwali is 8 November, 59 days out. The gifting window opens 5 October, 25 days out. The corporate pre-order deadline of 31 August has already passed. `my-work/growth-analyst/2026-09-10-brief.md` reports the Meta account dark for 22 days. The gifting creative was scheduled to re-light end of August and has not.

## Audience

- **Who:** two buyers who convert on the same visual. The gifter, from 6 VoC messages, buying for in-laws, a housewarming, a wedding or Diwali. And the corporate gifting buyer, from 1 message, buying 80 boxes for employees. The VoC report's finding is that these are one funnel, not two: the corporate order traced back to someone eating a paan at a colleague's wedding.
- **What they care about:** whether the box speaks before it is opened. Not one of the six gifting messages in the VoC set contains a complaint of any kind. They are not worried about the product. They are choosing a thing to be seen giving.
- **What stops them buying:** for the gifter, nothing on record. For the corporate buyer, one thing only, whether an 80 box order can actually be fulfilled.

## The three things the visual must contain

1. **The box, closed.** This is the whole brief. Every VoC quote in Theme 4 describes the closed box doing the work: "the box itself is a conversation starter", "festival in a box". Open the box in the photograph and the ad becomes a product shot like every other product shot.
2. **A blank sleeve.** Plain paper band around the box with nothing printed on it, or a name-shaped blank. It shows the 50+ box option without stating a price and it is what the corporate buyer is actually shopping for.
3. **More than one box.** Two for the gifter, a short stack of five for the corporate cut. Quantity is the difference between a purchase and an order.

## Tone

- **Three always-words from the voice rules:** name the thing rather than adjective it. Negate, then assert. First person singular, signed.
- **Three never-words:** gourmet, premium, authentic. Plus artisanal, elevate, unlock, and the deprecated natural and pure.
- **Mood in one line:** a parcel on a desk on a working Tuesday, not a hamper under a string of lights.
- **Reading level, for any text on the visual:** Class 8.

## What it must not show

From the anti-positioning:

- **No luxury gifting signals.** No gold foil, no gold ribbon, no marble, no velvet, no jewel-tone backdrop, no embossed monogram, no serif script. This is the exact look the four banned words describe, and the Market Analyst report confirms competitor copy leans on those adjectives across this category, which makes avoiding the look a real point of difference rather than a preference.
- **No hamper.** No basket, no shred filler, no cellophane, no assortment of other people's products. The wedge in `CLAUDE.md` S3 is that this is not a sweet shop that also sells paan, and a hamper photograph says the opposite.
- **No discount or urgency badge.** The brand never competes on price.

From the category's visual clichés, named for festival gifting specifically:

- **No diyas, no rangoli, no marigold garlands, no fairy lights, no sparklers, no firecrackers, no confetti, no bokeh.** Every competitor in the window is running some of these right now.
- **No betel leaf as garnish**, per "The leaf is leaf, not garnish."
- **No red stain, no spitting, no paan-shop counter, no tobacco tins, no zarda, no supari.**
- **No stock office.** If a desk appears it is a real desk with a real edge, not a rendered workspace with a succulent.
- **No hands presenting the box to camera.** The gift-being-handed-over shot is the single most used frame in Indian festival advertising.

## Craft references

- **Nicobar**, for how they photograph a closed object as the subject and let the packaging carry the whole frame. Study the flat neutral grounds and how little type they use. Not a competitor, not in food.
- **Good Earth**, for gift-object craft, specifically how they shoot a wrapped thing so the wrapping reads as considered rather than as decoration. Not a competitor, not in food.

## Image prompt

An eye-level photograph of a short stack of five closed kraft paper boxes resting on a plain wooden desk, shot straight on from the front at box height so the stack fills the lower two thirds of the frame. The boxes are simple uncoated brown kraft cartons, hand-packed and slightly imperfect at the corners, each wrapped with a narrow plain paper sleeve left entirely blank where a name would print, with the top box sitting very slightly out of alignment with the one below it. Setting is an ordinary working desk with a bare wall behind, no props, no plants, no decoration of any kind. Lighting is flat daylight from a window out of frame to the right, even and undramatic, with soft short shadows falling left. Composition is centred and frontal with clean empty wall above the stack for a short line of plain type, and the palette is limited to kraft brown, pale wall, and warm desk wood. Mood is plain, practical and quietly substantial, an order waiting to be sent rather than a gift being presented.

**Negative prompt:** no diyas, no oil lamps, no rangoli, no marigold garlands, no fairy lights, no sparklers, no firecrackers, no confetti, no bokeh lights, no gold foil, no gold ribbon, no silk bows, no marble, no velvet, no jewel-tone backdrop, no embossed monogram, no serif script or calligraphy, no hamper basket, no shred filler, no cellophane, no other brands' products, no open box, no visible food, no betel leaf as garnish, no red stain, no spitting, no paan-shop counter, no tobacco tins, no zarda, no supari, no hands presenting the box, no faces, no stock office, no succulents, no discount badge, no urgency banner, no text claiming any health, digestion, immunity or detox benefit.
