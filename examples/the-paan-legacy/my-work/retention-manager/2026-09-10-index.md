## Top five reads

1. **The moment most likely to lift repeat purchase this month: `whatsapp/replenishment.md`.** It is the only template in this run that is not new. The founder already ran it by hand, offering a three-time buyer "a calendar reminder, I ping you 5 days before the 6-week mark, no subscription friction, no auto-payment". The customer replied YES in two minutes. This template is that exact offer, made repeatable. It is the shortest path from a proven one-off to a system.

2. **The segment with the most reachable customers: `first-time-buyers`.** With repeat rate at 30%, roughly 70% of customers sit here at any moment. They have already answered the only question that was blocking them, whether this is spit-out paan, by eating it. The job with them is timing, not persuasion, which is a much easier job.

3. **The template most needing a founder voice check: `whatsapp/win-back.md`.** WhatsApp lands in the same thread as messages from someone's family, so it either sounds like Puneet or it should not send. This one opens by naming that the customer went quiet and asks whether something went wrong. Read it out loud. If it sounds like a brand, it fails.

4. **The highest-value segment with the message written for it: `repeat-buyers`, gone-quiet sub-state.** Repeat rate at 30% is the single strongest number in this business, corroborated independently by two sources. This is the segment that protects it. Note the behaviour recorded in the segment file: repeat buyers here **broaden rather than deepen**, three of eight bought a different SKU the second time. A replenishment message that only ever re-offers the same box under-serves them.

5. **Three flags block shipping.** In `whatsapp/win-back.md` the word "three times" is hardcoded in the body and will be wrong for most recipients, so it has to become a token. In `email/first-to-second-purchase.md` the line "most people buy a second thing within about six weeks" is stated as a pattern and rests on one customer. And `{fssai_license_number}` in the confirmation email is still missing, which is now the fifth deliverable it has blocked.

## The constraint that shaped every template here

**This brand cannot discount.** `CLAUDE.md` Section 3 says never compete on price. The standard win-back is a percentage off, the standard replenishment is a subscribe-and-save, and neither is available. So the win-back works on honesty instead, and explicitly says it is not sending a code. The replenishment works on timing alone. That is a harder brief and it is the correct one, and it also means **no template in this run carries a `{discount_value}` placeholder that someone could quietly fill in.**

## The timing assumption everything rests on

The 35 day replenishment trigger and the six week claim in the first-to-second email both come from **one customer**. From the customer research: "Bought from you three times now. Need a subscribe-and-save. Reordering manually every six weeks is annoying."

That is the only cadence figure that exists anywhere in this repo, and it contradicts the founder's own LTV model, which assumes 1.6 orders a year, roughly a 33 week gap. Six weeks and 33 weeks cannot both be right.

**Run the replenishment template against one cohort at 35 days and measure before rolling it out.** If the real gap is longer, this template arrives months early and trains people to ignore the channel.

## What I did not do

- **No live segment sizes.** The connected Shopify store is Anutilam Rice at anutilam.com, a different brand, and no data was read from it. Both segment files carry the filter but no count.
- **No festival trigger, and this is the notable gap.** The gifter persona's own card says what would bring them back is the festival calendar, and that there is no mechanism holding them between festivals. Diwali is 8 November. A dated reminder to prior gift buyers is plausibly worth more than every template in this run combined, and it is not one of the default five moments. Ask and it takes twenty minutes.
- **No cart abandon, browse abandon, VIP, complaint follow-up or third-order cross-sell.** Full run only.
- **No email flow definitions.** Full run only. The templates carry their own timing and stopping rules in the meantime.
- **Honest note on personalisation.** Everything here is token replacement, not per-customer writing. `{first_name}` in a WhatsApp message does not make it personal, and a customer who has had three real conversations with the founder will notice the difference immediately. Use these where no conversation exists, not on top of one.
- **Reading level left at Class 8 English, not simplified further.** The skill suggests simpler English where a base skews Tier-2 and Tier-3. This one does not. Every customer in the research is from Mumbai, Bengaluru, Delhi, Pune, Hyderabad, Chennai, Gurgaon or Kolkata.
