# Examples, two brands taken all the way through

Two fictional-but-realistic D2C brands, each run through all ten teammates. Same framework, deliberately different category shapes, so you can see the system hold rather than just take our word for it.

Open the one closest to your category first. Then open the other one, because the contrast is where the framework becomes visible.

| | [Little Lab](little-lab/) | [The Paan Legacy](the-paan-legacy/) |
|---|---|---|
| Category | Baby skincare | Gourmet paan and mukhwas |
| Compliance regime | CDSCO, cosmetics | FSSAI, food |
| Distribution | Amazon and Flipkart heavy | Own site, Zomato and Swiggy, plus franchise and corporate |
| Positioning | Paediatrician trust, ingredient transparency | Heritage meets modern, tobacco-free |
| Shape of the problem | A trust category with a nervous buyer | A ritual category with a lapsed habit |
| Scale | About ₹4.2Cr ARR | About ₹30L a month |

**Every number in both is illustrative.** Do not quote either in a real deck.

---

## How to use these

**When a session's instructions are clear but you cannot picture the output.** Open the matching folder in the brand closest to yours. Every session file links straight to the right one.

**When you are running behind.** Copy the relevant subfolder into your own `my-work/` and carry on. You lose personalisation for that one teammate, but the chain stays alive so the next session still has something to read. Better a borrowed input than a broken chain.

**When you want to see how a teammate handles your compliance regime.** Little Lab is a cosmetics brand and Paan Legacy is food, so between them they cover most of what an Indian D2C brand runs into. Read the claim wall in either `CLAUDE.md` Section 7.

## What is in each brand

```
<brand>/
├── CLAUDE.md              the filled brand profile, the output of Session 1
├── brand-brain/           the inputs that produced it
│   ├── positioning.md     competitors.md     products.csv
│   ├── reviews.md         unit-economics.md  nominated-skus.md
│   ├── ads/               past ad copy with performance notes
│   └── voice-dna/         the founder's own writing, the highest value folder
├── sample-inputs/         raw exports: Shopify CSVs, ad spend, reviews, support threads
└── my-work/               what all ten teammates produced
```

**Read `brand-brain/` before `my-work/`.** The interesting question is not what the teammates wrote, it is what they were given. Compare `brand-brain/voice-dna/` against the tone of anything in `my-work/content-lead/` and you can see the transfer happen.

## The Paan Legacy has two dated runs

This is worth knowing before it confuses you.

`the-paan-legacy/my-work/` contains work from **two different weeks**, May 2026 and September 2026. That is not a mistake, it is the point.

- The **May** run is the brand's first pass, straight after the bootcamp.
- The **September** run is four months later, on the same brand, with better data and a live ad account connected.

Compare the two Voice of Customer reports, or the two growth briefs. The September ones are sharper because the inputs got better, not because the teammates changed. **That is what compounding actually looks like**, and it is the argument for re-running these every week rather than once at a bootcamp.

The growth analyst archive at `my-work/growth-analyst/data/archive/` holds both weeks' numbers, which is how the brief eventually gets to show trends instead of snapshots.

## A word on honesty in these examples

Both brands' outputs are full of flags, gaps and refusals. Missing FSSAI numbers. A store connection pointing at the wrong brand. Sample sizes called out as too small. A skill declining to tick a gluten-free box because nothing in the profile states it.

**None of that is failure, and none of it was cleaned up for display.** It is what the system is supposed to do. An example run with no flags in it would teach you the wrong lesson about what good output looks like.
